The Echo Panel: why this question, what exists, what we add

AI products increasingly answer with a panel of advisors. People read a panel's agreement as corroboration. We measured how much independent evidence that agreement actually carries, what over-counting it costs, and who over-counts.

One-line answer. Five personas of one model carry about 1.1 independent opinions, five different models about 1.5, and one round of discussion takes it back to 1.1 while unanimity rises from 56% to 84% with no gain in accuracy. AI readers average; people are the ones at risk of counting.

Prior work: what is measured, and what is missing

WorkWhat it showsWhat it leaves open
Goldman 2001 (Phil. & Phenom. Research)Agreeing experts add evidence only if their opinions are not derived from each otherNo measurement for AI advisors
Clemen & Winkler 1985 (Operations Research)Hard limits on the information from dependent sourcesClassic forecasting setting
Lorenz et al. 2011; Becker et al. 2017 (PNAS)Social influence narrows human crowds without improving them; network structure mattersHumans only
Kim et al. 2025 (ICML); Goel et al. 2025 (ICML)LLMs from different developers share mistakes; similarity grows with capabilityBenchmark accuracy, not product panels or forecasting under uncertainty
Schoenegger et al. 2024 (Science Advances)A 12-model average matched a human crowd on 31 questionsDid not measure independence or the reader
Aitchison et al. 2026; Begin et al. 2026; Douven 2026Frontier forecasters are highly correlated; ten tuned agents ≈ 1.4 forecasters; contamination inflates resultsAccuracy-oriented ensembles, not personas, discussion or the human reader
Lee & Lee 2026 (N = 127)A unanimous set of agents inflates users' confidenceNo measure of how much evidence the agreement carried
Hussain & Nielbo 2026; Huang et al. 2026LLM agents conform to majorities; post-communication agreement overstates reliabilityBenchmarks with known answers; no human-crowd comparison

Alternatives we considered

  • Multi-agent conformity (FlipRate in a group). Six or more 2026 papers already measure it, including one with a million graded responses.
  • Planted false sources for research agents. DRNOISE, MisKnow-Agent and the Synthetic Web benchmark cover it well.
  • Hedges lost across agent handoffs. "Silence Is Endorsement" and "Manufactured Confidence" (2026) cover much of it.
  • Dispute-escalation benchmark. Strongly human-centred and close to Stray's talk, but a listed example project, so likely crowded.

We chose the Echo Panel because the human-facing link (how much a panel's agreement should move a reader) had no measurement, and because it builds on our previous sprint work on agent swarms.

Risks and how we handled them

RiskHandling
Contamination (models know the answers)Only questions frozen and resolved after every model's training cutoff
The wrong statisticRaw error correlation is near 1 for any panel with yes/no outcomes; we use correlation within outcome classes and show the difference with a synthetic test
Leakage into the labelLabel values computed on the other four-fifths of questions
A strawman readerThe five-witness reading is stated as a stylised upper bound; the human claim rests on cited studies and a preregistered protocol
Unreliable open-weight endpointsMain results on OpenAI models; cross-developer panel as a robustness check on the questions it completed
Dual use (false labels, gaming the statistic)Shown directly with the inflated-label arm; label must come from a public track record and a party other than the vendor (report, Appendix A)
